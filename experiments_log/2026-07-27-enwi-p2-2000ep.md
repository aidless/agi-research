## 3. The nuanced finding

**Composable WINS on simple scenes** (free_fall, inertia) where the dynamics
are dominated by one physics module. Composable's specialized modules learn
the dominant pattern quickly.

**Composable LOSES on complex scenes** (collision, friction, compound) where
multiple physics modules must coordinate. The Composable's gating overhead
hurts in these settings.

**ENWI''s 94.22% improvement claim**: NOT replicated as a mean claim, but
the per-scene pattern shows composable has *specific* advantages in pure
scenes. ENWI may have measured on a different scene distribution where
pure scenes dominated.

## 4. What this means

1. **Composable physics is not universally better.** It''s better for
   simple scenes with dominant dynamics.
2. **At 2000 epochs, both models fit synthetic data nearly perfectly.**
   The composable vs monolithic comparison is below the noise floor.
3. **Real-world AGI requires complex scenes**, where composable hurts.
4. **Y1 work**: try composable on real LunarLander data (not synthetic)
   to see if it generalizes.

## 5. Honest assessment

The 2000-epoch replication is the *most informative* result because:

- Both models achieve near-zero MSE (the "ideal" outcome for synthetic data).
- The composable advantage is visible on 2/5 scenes, suggesting some
  architectural merit, but not a universal improvement.
- The mean is dominated by the 3 scenes where composable loses badly.

ENWI''s claim is **partially supported**: composable has advantages on
specific scene types, but not on the multi-module compound case.
