## 4. What this means

### 4.1 What Y1.3 ACTUALLY shows (final)
- **Reward shaping during PPO training (any signal source) helps
  PPO on LunarLander-v3 by ~+16-40 mean reward.** This is a
  real regularization effect.
- The Monitor signal does NOT add statistically significant value
  above shaping. The n=5 result that suggested +22-25 was within
  sampling noise; n=15 confirms.
- The contribution is **"PPO + reward shaping helps"**, not
  "Monitor architecture provides useful signal."

### 4.2 What the v1.2 result (n=5) was wrong about
- The +22-25 delta (real vs random) was based on n=5 controls
- The CI on this delta was wide; t=0.78 with n=15 confirms the
  effect is not robust
- This is a textbook example of the "pilot study overclaims"
  pattern that the knowledge base critique warned about

### 4.3 What the pre-registration SAVED us from
If we had skipped the pre-registration and used the n=5 result,
we would have published "+22-25 Monitor signal effect" as a
publishable claim. With n=15, we see the effect is not significant.

This validates the NO_SELF_DECEPTION.md P0 rule: "extend to n=10+
before claiming significant."

## 6. What I learned (for future work)

1. **Pre-registration works.** Stating the hypothesis and decision
   rule BEFORE the data made the verdict unambiguous.
2. **n=5 is too small for paired comparisons.** Even n=15 gives
   t=0.78 for a delta of 13.6. Real effects may exist but require
   much larger samples to confirm.
3. **Self-deception compounds.** v1.0 -> v1.1 -> v1.2 (n=5) ->
   v1.2 (n=15) is a sequence of increasingly honest claims. Each
   step required running more controls. The right move would have
   been to run all 3 controls at n=15 from the start.
4. **The "novel contribution" shifted:** the original v1.0 claimed
   "Monitor helps PPO". The final v1.2 says "Monitor does not
   specifically help PPO; reward shaping does". This is a less
   exciting claim but it is honest.
