## 6. Why Frozen == Joint at N=6

With only 4 training traces and 2 eval traces, both Frozen and Joint
arms achieve perfect classification on the eval set. This is a
**ceiling effect** at small N: when the task is easy and the data is
small, both arms reach AUROC 1.0.

The H10 hypothesis ("frozen > joint by delta > 0.05") cannot be
**distinguished** from a tie at this scale. To test the hypothesis,
we need either:
1. **Larger N** (more traces, more seeds) where one arm can be
   better than the other.
2. **Harder traces** where the failure mode is harder to learn and
   the decoupling advantage becomes visible.
3. **More failure classes** (multi-class failure instead of binary)
   to differentiate arm quality.
