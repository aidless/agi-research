## 3. Why still t<2.0

The trained arms have HIGH variance (std=48-59). This is real
variance, not noise:
  - H2.0-A trained: std=59.0 (range 4.5 to 175.0)
  - H2.0-B trained: std=48.3 (range 32.0 to 171.0)

Some seeds give huge gains (175, 171), some give modest gains
(36, 32), some give near-zero (4.5, 33). The trained model
sometimes helps a lot, sometimes barely helps. This is HIGH
run-to-run variance.

To reach t=2.0 with std=50-60 and delta=+30, we would need:
  n = (z * sigma / delta)^2 = (2 * 55 / 30)^2 = 14 seeds per arm

So n=10 is below the power threshold. n=15-20 per arm would
be needed for t=2.0 to reach significance.

Combined: Both new direction tests show direction-positive effects

**RECOMMENDATION**:

## 5. What this means for the paper

The H2.0 tests now have n=10 evidence:
- Forward model exploration: direction=+32.9, NOT significant
- Simple MLP reward shaping: direction=+29.1, NOT significant

The paper should report BOTH the direction and the verdict. The
direction is informative (suggests the abstraction is useful)
but the verdict per the rule is honest (NOT supported).

This is a "robust null result" - the direction is consistent but
the variance is too high for significance. It is a meaningful
negative result, not a "single negative result" - the direction
is stable across 2 test setups (n=5 and n=10).

## 6. The deeper issue: trained arms have high variance

The trained arms (FM, MLP) have much higher std (48-59) than the
random arms (28-46). This is because:
- The trained model sometimes learns a useful representation
  (giving +50 to +100 gain)
- The trained model sometimes learns a noise representation
  (giving 0 to +30 gain)
- The random model is consistent in giving 0 to +20 noise

The trained model's VARIANCE is the problem, not its mean.
This is a well-known issue: learned auxiliary signals have
high run-to-run variance.
